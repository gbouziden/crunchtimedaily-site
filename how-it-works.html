How it works

Crunch Time Daily puts you on the sideline for the last minute of a real, close NBA game. You make every call, and it actually plays out using the real players' season numbers, so the game can go differently than it really did. At the end you see your result next to the real game's. Every call is graded against the best option from that exact spot.

The endings

Each ending is a real game: every close finish from the last ten regular seasons, plus close playoff games back to 2001 and a hand-picked set of Greatest Endings. You get the real score, clock, timeouts for both teams, each team's record going in (or the series score in the playoffs), and the real teams' season shooting. You coach one side: a comeback, protecting a lead, or a tied game with the last shot. In Greatest Endings you coach the team that made the famous play.

Making the call

On offense you pick a play (attack the rim, spot-up three, star isolation, or pick-and-roll), a tempo (quick, a normal set, or hold for the last shot), and whether to burn a timeout to advance the ball. On defense you pick how to guard it: play it straight, run them off the three-point line, trap the ball, or foul on purpose.

Defensive looks

Before each possession you see what the defense is showing: switching everything, dropping the big, running you off the line, trapping the pick-and-roll, or packing the paint. Each look changes which calls work. A switch creates mismatches for your star, a drop leaves pull-ups open, a packed paint leaves shooters open. On defense you see their offensive lean instead, such as hunting a three or going to their star. The looks are modeled; play-by-play doesn't record them.

How calls are graded

Every play is simulated with the real five on the floor: each player's real 3-point, 2-point and free-throw percentages from that season. You choose who takes the shot. Before the result is shown, the game simulates the rest of the game 2,000 times for every option you had, using late-game NBA shooting rates adjusted for the two real teams. Your call's win chance is compared with the best option's:

  • 🟩 Best call, or within 2% of it
  • 🟨 Close, within 5%
  • 🟧 Costly, within 10%
  • 🟥 Blunder

The result of the play doesn't change the grade. A good call that rims out is still a good call. Late-game basketball has a lot of near-ties, and when several calls are within a point of each other, the game says so.

Beating the real coach

The real coach's call on each possession is read from the play-by-play and mapped to the closest option: a three, a drive, a pull-up, a timeout, an intentional foul. It's graded the same way you are. At the end you see your grade next to theirs, how you rank against everyone who played today, how you compare with every real NBA coach in these spots, and which coach your calls look most like.

Survival

Random real endings, back to back. Match or beat the real coach's grade to keep going. The first time the coach outgrades you, the run is over. Set a nickname to get your best run on the Survival leaderboard.

The data

Scores, clocks, timeouts and plays come from public play-by-play at Basketball-Reference.com, rewritten in our own words. Team shooting comes from each season's team stats. No NBA logos or marks are used.
